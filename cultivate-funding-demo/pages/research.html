Why Questioning Matters

Research-backed principles driving our approach

📚

CLASS Framework

The Classroom Assessment Scoring System (CLASS) identifies teacher-child interactions as the primary mechanism through which early childhood programs influence child development. Quality questioning is a key indicator of Instructional Support.

Pianta, R. C., La Paro, K. M., & Hamre, B. K. (2008)
🧠

Cognitive Development

Open-ended questions promote higher-order thinking skills, vocabulary development, and language production. Children who experience more open-ended questioning show greater gains in cognitive and language assessments.

Wasik, B. A., & Hindman, A. H. (2011)
⏱️

Wait Time Research

Studies show that increasing wait time to 3-5 seconds after asking a question leads to longer and more complex student responses, increased student-to-student discourse, and higher-level thinking.

Rowe, M. B. (1986); Tobin, K. (1987)
💬

Sustained Shared Thinking

Extended interactions where adults and children work together on a cognitive challenge are associated with better developmental outcomes. Open-ended questions are a key strategy for initiating these interactions.

Siraj-Blatchford, I. (2009)

OEQ vs CEQ Comparison

Understanding the difference in practice

Open-Ended Questions (OEQ)
Closed-Ended Questions (CEQ)

Definition

Questions that invite multiple possible responses and encourage elaboration, thinking, and exploration.

Definition

Questions with a limited set of acceptable answers, often yes/no or single-word responses.

Example Starters

  • "What do you think..."
  • "How did you..."
  • "Why do you..."
  • "Tell me about..."
  • "What would happen if..."

Example Starters

  • "Is it..."
  • "Are you..."
  • "Did you..."
  • "Can you..."
  • "Do you want..."

Benefits

  • Promotes critical thinking
  • Develops vocabulary
  • Encourages creativity
  • Builds confidence
  • Supports language development

When Appropriate

  • Quick comprehension checks
  • Safety confirmations
  • Routine transitions
  • Factual recall
  • Building to open-ended follow-ups

Model Training Process

How we built and validated our classification system

1

Data Collection

26 classroom videos collected from diverse early childhood settings across Washington State, representing infant/toddler through Pre-K age groups.

2

Expert Annotation

Cultivate Learning researchers annotated 119 educator questions with OEQ/CEQ labels, timestamp information, and contextual notes about wait time and child responses.

3

Feature Engineering

56 linguistic features extracted including question word patterns, sentence structure, length metrics, and semantic indicators based on CLASS research.

4

Model Training

7 diverse classifiers trained: Neural Network, Random Forest, XGBoost, SVM, Logistic Regression, LightGBM, and Gradient Boosting. 5-fold cross-validation used.

5

Ensemble Integration

Soft voting ensemble combines all 7 models with optimized weights. Achieves 89% accuracy with balanced precision/recall for both classes.

6

Human-in-the-Loop

Continuous improvement through feedback collection. Educators can mark classifications as correct/incorrect to expand training data.
